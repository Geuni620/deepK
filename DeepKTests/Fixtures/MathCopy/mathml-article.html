1. Recipes Scale by ×k, Pans by k2

A loaf needs 500 g of flour and 10 g of salt. To bake for a crowd you multiply every line by the same factor (k), so k=3 gives 1500 g and 30 g.

Bakers keep the dough ratio wf fixed, where w is water and f is flour. A ratio of 1320 is a soft dough; anything above 45 is closer to batter.

A round pan of radius r holds πr2 of batter per centimetre of depth, so a pan twice as wide needs four times the dough. Its diameter is d=2A/π, and going the other way, the radius for a target area A is

r=Aπ

With several pans p1,…,pn the total area is ∑i=1nπri2, and the scale factor is that sum divided by the area of the original pan:

k=1A0∑i=1nπri2

If batch i uses i cups, the first n batches use ∑i=1ni=n(n+1)2 cups in total.

Two flours can be tracked per batch as a grid (300200450300) with one row per batch, and scaling only the second batch is the matrix

S=(100k)

x marks the starting weight in this paragraph, and t in minutes keeps its label as text, and a pan tilted by θ fills to a depth of hcos⁡θ along its wall.

Proofing time does not scale with the batch size, which is the one rule that surprises most home bakers.
